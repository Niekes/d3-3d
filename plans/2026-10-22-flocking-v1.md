# Flocking simulation example

## Objective

Add a docs example, "Flocking", at `/examples/flocking`. A flock of 150 boids flies in a box. Every boid is a dot drawn with `points3D`, with a short tail drawn with `lines3D` that shows where it is heading.

- **Three rules:** separation, alignment and cohesion, after Craig Reynolds' boids, each with a weight slider, plus a slider for the radius a boid sees its neighbors in.
- **Count:** a picker for 50, 150 or 300 boids.
- **Speed colors:** every boid is colored by its speed on a sequential scale, with a legend.
- **Perspective:** a checkbox moves the eye from infinitely far away to 4 box units from the middle.
- **Order readout:** the polarization, from 0 for a swarm that flies every way to 1 for a flock that flies as one, shows what the alignment slider does.
- **Playback:** the scaffold button plays and pauses, and a reset button restores the starting flock.
- **Rotating and keyboard:** dragging rotates the scene with the shared glide, and the keys play, pause and step.

The page teaches three things:

1. **The projection moves points, the figure sizes marks.** In the default orthographic view a boid far away looks exactly as large as one nearby. `points3D` hands every point its rotated position as its centroid (`src/shapes/points.ts:20`), and its z is the depth. The figure scales the radius and the opacity of each dot by that depth, which gives the flock its depth back. With perspective on, the projection draws the far boids closer together, but a circle's radius is still the figure's, so it grows by the same factor as the projection, which the perspective street computes as `growth` at `docs/examples/perspectiveStreet.ts:80-81`. This is the main lesson.
2. **Simulate in the model, project per frame.** The boids live in a box from −1 to 1. Every frame steps the simulation, projects the same data again with `.data`, joins the dots by id and paints them back to front with `sort`, without a transition.
3. **Three local rules make a flock.** No boid knows the flock, only its neighbors, and the polarization readout shows order appear from alignment alone.

Assumptions:

- **No library changes.** `points3D`, `lines3D`, `axes3D`, `axisSpec`, `camera3D` and `sort` are public.
- **Perspective is a toggle.** The page opens orthographic, at the default distance of `Infinity`. The checkbox sets the distance to 4 box units. The box reaches √3 from its middle, so every boid stays in front of the eye, as the perspective street keeps its scene inside its nearest eye at `docs/examples/perspectiveStreet.ts:19-20`.
- **The box and the speeds.** Positions stay within −1 to 1 on every axis. Speeds stay between 0.15 and 0.6 box units per second. The neighbor radius runs from 0.1 to 0.5 with 0.25 as default, and separation acts within half of it.
- **The neighbor search.** Every pair is tested, so 300 boids are 45,000 pairs per frame. The code marks this with a `ponytail:` comment that names a uniform grid as the upgrade.
- **Seeded randomness.** The starting flock comes from the d3 `randomLcg` generator with a fixed seed, so reset restores the same flock.
- **The time step.** A step never takes more than 1/30 of a second, so a tab that comes back from the background does not fling the flock.
- **Shared rules.** All `AGENTS.md` rules apply. The styles live in the module, as in `docs/examples/splineEditor.ts:455`.

## Implementation Plan

- [ ] 1. Create `docs/examples/flocking.ts`, which exports only `mountFlocking`, taking the host and returning a dispose function.
  - Mount it through `rotatingFigure` at `docs/examples/rotatingFigure.ts:155` with the prefix `flock`.

  - Keep the state in one place: the boids with their id, position and velocity, the three weights, the radius, the count, the yaw and the pitch. One render function reads the state.

  Rationale: this is the house pattern of every rotating figure.

- [ ] 2. Seed the flock from the seeded generator: positions uniform in a ball of radius 0.6, velocities in random directions at the middle speed. Changing the count seeds a new flock and keeps the weights. Rationale: reset and the count picker both start from a known flock.

- [ ] 3. Write the step function in two passes, so the order of the array never biases the flock.
  - **First pass, for every boid:** collect the neighbors within the radius, and sum three steering terms.
  - **Separation:** away from every neighbor within half the radius, weighted by the inverse square of the distance plus a small epsilon.
  - **Alignment:** towards the mean velocity of the neighbors.
  - **Cohesion:** towards the mean position of the neighbors.
  - **Walls:** within 0.15 of a wall, a push back inside that grows with the depth into the margin.
  - **Second pass:** add the weighted sum to every velocity, clamp the speed between the two limits and move every boid by its velocity.

  Rationale: the walls steer instead of wrapping, because a boid that jumps across a 3D box is hard to follow.

- [ ] 4. Render the scene in two groups.
  - **Walls:** the back walls of an `axes3D` box from −1 to 1, its spec built with `axisSpec` as at `docs/examples/trajectoryExplorer.ts:80-84`, drawn first, as `docs/examples/trajectoryExplorer.ts:181` filters them. The filter by `ccw` holds in perspective too.
  - **Flock:** the dots as circles and the tails as lines from each boid back along its velocity by 0.15 seconds, placed with `placeSegments` from `docs/examples/segments.ts:8`.

  Join the dots and the tails by the boid id, and sort both in one painter's list as `docs/examples/meshInspector.ts:199` does. Rationale: a tail behind its dot is painted first and stays under it.

- [ ] 5. Give the flock its depth back from the depth of each dot, the z of its centroid.

  - **Both:** map the depth onto an opacity from 0.45 to 1. The tail takes the opacity of its dot.

  Rationale: this is the main lesson, and √3 is the half-diagonal of the box, the deepest a point in it can lie.

## Verification Criteria

- The gates pass, and `git diff --stat -- index.ts src tests` is empty.
- **Alignment:** with alignment at 2 and the others at 1, the polarization rises above 0.8 within 20 seconds. With alignment at 0, it stays below 0.4.
- **Separation and cohesion:** with separation at 0 the boids clump into overlapping dots, and with cohesion at 0 the flock spreads through the box.
- **Walls:** over two minutes at any setting, no boid leaves the box.
- **Depth:** at every rotation and in both projections, the dot nearest the viewer is the largest and the most opaque, and a dot that passes behind another is painted under it.
- **Perspective:** with perspective on, the radii of any two dots stand in the ratio of their growth factors, and the dots stay round.
- **Colors:** the ends of the legend match the slowest and the fastest boids.
- **Reset:** two resets in a row give the same first frame.
- **Frame time:** with 300 boids, a step and a render together stay under 16 ms on a laptop.
- **Keyboard:** Space and the period key work, and the live region announces the state.
- **Reduced motion:** nothing moves until the reader presses play.
- **Clean unmount:** no console errors and no running timer after leaving the page mid-play or mid-glide.
- **Themes and width:** light and dark themes are legible, and at 375 px nothing overlaps.

## Potential Risks and Mitigations

1. **Testing every pair is too slow at 300 boids.**
   Mitigation: 45,000 distance checks take about a millisecond. If the frame time criterion fails, add the uniform grid named in the `ponytail:` comment.

2. **Two boids on the same spot make the separation term infinite.**
   Mitigation: the epsilon in the denominator from step 3, and the speed clamp after it.

3. **Extreme weights make the flock collapse into a point or oscillate.**
   Mitigation: the speed clamp bounds every boid, and the sliders stop at 2. Check every slider at both ends.

4. **Sorting 600 elements every frame costs too much.**
   Mitigation: measure it. If it fails the frame time criterion, sort only the dots and paint all tails in a group beneath them.

5. **The speed ramp is hard to read on one of the page colors.**
   Mitigation: clip the ramp, as step 6 says, and check both themes.

## Alternative Approaches

1. **A canvas or WebGL figure through `docs/examples/canvasFigure.ts:50`:** thousands of boids, but it needs the grid search, and it loses the keyed join that the page shows.
2. **Walls that wrap around:** less code, but a boid that leaves on one side jumps to the other, which is hard to follow in 3D.
3. **Boids as small cones of triangles:** the heading shows better, but at four times the elements, and the tails already show it.
